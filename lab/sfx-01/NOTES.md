# Reference: when to use which sound effect

Reel by Anderson Tai (@anderson.taii, "these are my top 5 most used sound effects"), 12.3 s.
Client brief: ignore the visuals; take the rules he explains for when to use which sound.

## What he says (transcribed)
"Use the click sound effect for when things pop up on the screen. Use the flash sound effect for
quick cuts. Use the whoosh sound effect when you want to transition from scene to scene. Use the
glitch sound effect whenever you want text to pop up on the screen. And use the riser when you want
to build tension."

## Where the sounds sit in his own demo (audio vs picture)
- click: a short click on each element as it pops up ("pop up", "on the", "screen" at 1.5-2.3 s).
- flash: a broadband hit exactly on the cut (4.17 s).
- whoosh: builds over ~0.5 s and peaks on the transition (blur cut at 7.0 s).
- glitch: a short, dense high-frequency burst when the big "TEXT" appears (~8.9-9.1 s).
- riser: energy rising over the last ~2 s towards the end.

## Mapped onto our edits
| Moment in our edit | Sound |
|---|---|
| a caption appears | the client's camera shutter (client rule; replaces "click" for captions) |
| an element pops up on a canvas (card, icon, number, timeline point) | click |
| a quick cut / view switch (incl. negative-flash switches) | flash, on the cut frame |
| scene change: into or out of canvas mode, whip transitions | whoosh, peaking on the cut |
| a big text graphic (canvas title, big keyword) | glitch, starting on the frame it appears |
| build-up into a key point or reveal | riser, peaking on the moment |
