# Reference: speaker shrinks into a panel, visual canvas explains what he says

Reel by Kallaway (@kallawaymarketing, "Application Content"), 91 s, 1080x1920, 24 fps.
Client brief: when he shrinks and a small part of his body is cut out and overlaps a canvas on
which what he explains is visualised. Use this "here and there", so visuals represent what the
client is saying. Ignore its captions.

## Measured / observed
- Two modes. Full-screen talking head, and "canvas mode". The switch is a HARD CUT on a phrase start
  (no animated shrink in this reel); canvas mode holds for several seconds while he explains
  something visual, then cuts back to full screen.
- Canvas mode layout (1080x1920):
  - background: near-black with a faint dot grid
  - title at the top: 2 lines, bold white sans, centred
  - the visual in the middle: cards with rounded corners and a thin white border, timelines with red
    dots, branch diagrams, phone screenshots / b-roll in cards
  - a one-word label under the visual
  - the speaker in a rounded panel at the bottom (about 87% of the width, bottom ~27% of the frame),
    framed wider than the full-screen shot
  - his head is cut out and pops above the panel's top edge, overlapping the canvas
- The visual moves with his words: the title types in word by word, the timeline slides (with
  motion blur) to the year he says, cards appear as he mentions them.

## How it maps to the client's videos
- On sentences that explain something visual: cut to canvas mode; the client in the bottom panel
  with the head cut out over the edge (subject masks already work); above, a visual built for that
  sentence (diagram, timeline, list, number, comparison, screenshot or b-roll the client provides),
  animated in sync with the forced-aligned words.
- In and out of canvas mode: a hard cut, combinable with the 1-2 frame negative flash from the
  transitions reference.
